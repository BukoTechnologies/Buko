Configured isn’t the same as enforced

A security review is really two problems wearing one hat: working out what’s genuinely true today, and fixing the parts you’d rather not admit to. SteelCurtain does both — starting with the part almost nobody does properly.

Answer it honestly

Forward us the form — whatever shape it arrived in — and connect your accounts. Every question gets mapped to something we have actually checked, so you get a line-by-line readout instead of a blank document and a bad feeling. Each answer carries its evidence and the date we confirmed it.

Check what actually holds

Reading a setting tells you what someone intended. We work out what an attacker would find: which identities can still reach what, which policies cancel each other out, which old protocol is quietly still answering. Three escalating levels of proof, described below.

Close the gaps

Everything we find comes back as a prioritised list in plain English: what is actually broken, what it would take to fix, and whether it is worth your afternoon. Most of it turns out to be already sitting in the tools you pay for every month, switched off.

Keep it true

An honest answer has a shelf life. Policies get loosened to fix a login problem; admin accounts outlive the admin. We keep re-checking every claim you signed your name to and tell you the day one stops being accurate — so the next form starts at mostly-done instead of blank.

Three ways we check

Each one is harder evidence than the last. Most tools stop at the first.

1
Read the setting

Query the configuration and see what it says. Is the policy on, is the box ticked, is the feature licensed. This is necessary, it is cheap, and it is where every other tool in this category stops — which is why so many confident questionnaire answers are wrong.

2
Look for the way around

Access policies form a system, and systems have gaps that no individual rule reveals. We take the whole policy set and work out whether any identity, in any situation, still reaches what it shouldn’t — the excluded group, the trusted location, the legacy protocol, the service principal nobody has looked at since setup.

3
Actually try it

The strongest evidence there is. With your explicit permission, on a schedule you pick, we attempt the bypass ourselves against an account we created for the purpose — and record exactly what happened. Never against your staff, never anything destructive. Just a timestamped answer nobody can wave away.

What SteelCurtain isn’t

Security vendors have a habit of implying they cover everything. Here is a straight list of what we don’t do, so nobody finds out the awkward way:

  • Not antivirus, not endpoint detection — nothing gets installed on anyone’s laptop
  • Not a SIEM, and not a 24/7 monitoring service with people watching screens
  • Not backup, disaster recovery, or incident response
  • Not a penetration test, and not a substitute for one
  • Not a SOC 2 audit — we’ll help you answer the questions, but we’re not your auditor

What we do is narrow on purpose: know what’s genuinely true about your security, prove it, and keep it that way. The same record answers your next customer’s review — and your insurance renewal, which asks most of the same things.
