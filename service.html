Present isn’t the same as exploitable

A product security review is two problems wearing one hat: working out what is genuinely true about the thing you ship, and proving it to somebody who has every reason to be sceptical. Winnow does both — starting with the part almost nobody does properly.

Answer it honestly

Send us the questionnaire in whatever shape it arrived and point us at your build. Every question gets mapped to something we have actually verified, so you get a line-by-line readout instead of a blank document and a lost week. Each answer carries its evidence and the release it was confirmed against.

Prove what’s exploitable

The core of it. We take your SBOM, match it against known vulnerabilities, then do the part everyone skips: work out which of those are genuinely reachable in the artefact you ship. The output is a VEX document your customer’s security team can verify rather than take on faith.

Close the real gaps

Whatever survives the reachability pass is a genuine finding, and those come back ranked by exposure rather than by CVSS score. A handful of things worth an afternoon, instead of four hundred rows that make everyone give up and start hand-waving.

Keep it true as you ship

For a product, an honest answer has a very short shelf life — every release changes the SBOM and the CVE picture underneath it. Winnow runs from your pipeline, so the answers move with the build and the next customer gets something current instead of something from four releases ago.

Three ways we check

Each is harder evidence than the last.

1
Read the manifest

Parse the SBOM, the build configuration, the fleet and cloud setup, and see what they claim. Necessary and cheap. It is also the entire depth of most tooling in this space, which is exactly why a scary-looking CVE list gets treated as a scary product.

2
Work out what’s reachable

A component being present in a manifest says almost nothing about whether anyone can get to it. We look at what actually made it into the shipped artefact, whether the vulnerable code sits on a path anything can call, and whether the interface it needs is even exposed on your device. The same question applies to your backend: can one customer’s account reach another customer’s fleet?

3
Actually try it

The strongest evidence there is. With your permission, on hardware and environments you nominate, we attempt the thing rather than reasoning about it — push an unsigned update and confirm it is refused, capture what the device genuinely transmits, probe the tenant boundary. Timestamped results nobody has to take on trust.

What Winnow isn’t

Security vendors have a habit of implying they cover everything. Here is a straight list of what we don’t do, so nobody finds out the awkward way:

  • Not a firmware binary analysis platform — if you already run one, we read its output rather than competing with it
  • Not a SAST or DAST tool, and not a code scanner
  • Not a penetration test, and not a substitute for one
  • Not an agent that runs on your devices in the field
  • Not a certification body — we’ll help you answer the questions, but we’re not your auditor

What we do is narrow on purpose: know what is genuinely true about the product you ship, prove it in a form somebody else can check, and keep it current as you release. The same verified record answers the next customer’s review — and increasingly, the regulators asking the same questions.
