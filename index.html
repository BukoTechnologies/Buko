The deal is basically done. Then their security team sends the product questionnaire — data flows, firmware signing, what your cloud can reach inside their network, what happens when you find a vulnerability. Two hundred questions about a machine, not a spreadsheet.

What Winnow does

Get the deal unblocked. Stay unblocked as you ship. That’s the whole product.

Answer the whole form

Every question gets an answer. The ones we can prove come with the proof attached — evidence, timestamp, and the build it came from.

Prove what’s exploitable

Your SBOM lists hundreds of CVEs. We work out which are genuinely reachable in the code you ship, and produce the VEX to back it up.

Close the real gaps

What’s actually broken, ranked by whether anyone could reach it. A short list you can act on, not a wall of red.

Keep it true as you ship

Every release changes the answer. We re-verify from your build pipeline, so the questionnaire never goes stale between customers.

Three ways we check

Each one is harder evidence than the last. Most tools stop at the first.

1
Read the manifest

Parse the SBOM, the build configuration, the cloud and fleet setup. Necessary, cheap, and nowhere near proof — but it’s where almost everything else in this category stops.

2
Work out what’s reachable

Is the vulnerable function actually in the binary you shipped, on a path anything can call? Can one customer’s account reach another customer’s devices? Presence is not exposure, and the difference is most of your CVE list.

3
Actually try it

Push an unsigned update and watch it get rejected. Capture what the device really sends, rather than what the design doc says it sends. Test the isolation instead of describing it.

Four hundred CVEs, and maybe six that matter

Every product security review now asks for an SBOM, and every SBOM comes back looking like a disaster. It almost never is. The vulnerable version is in there, but:

  • The affected component was compiled out of the build you actually ship
  • The vulnerable function exists but nothing in your product ever calls it
  • It’s only reachable through an interface your device doesn’t expose
  • It needs local access to a machine nobody can log into
  • You already mitigated it somewhere else entirely, and never wrote that down

Right now you defend all of that by hand, in email, every single time. Winnow works out which CVEs are genuinely reachable and produces a defensible VEX document that says so — so “trust us, it’s fine” becomes something the customer’s security team can actually check.

Four hundred rows in, six that matter out. Hence the name.
