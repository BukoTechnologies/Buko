Three ways we check

Most tools stop at the first one.

1
Read the setting

The obvious part: is the policy switched on? Necessary, and nowhere near sufficient. This is where everyone else stops.

2
Look for the way around

Your access policies interact in ways nobody can hold in their head. We work through the whole set and find the paths that slip past — the excluded group, the legacy protocol, the service account.

3
Actually try it

With your say-so, we attempt the bypass ourselves against an account we control, and record what happened. Evidence nobody can argue with.

“MFA is enabled” and “MFA can’t be bypassed” are different sentences

Every questionnaire asks whether you enforce multi-factor authentication. Almost everyone says yes. Plenty of them are wrong, because the console says enabled while something quietly routes around it:

  • A policy exclusion added for one contractor in 2023 that nobody removed
  • A legacy protocol still accepting username and password, no second factor required
  • A service account with standing access and no interactive login to prompt on
  • A break-glass account that quietly became somebody’s daily driver
  • Someone disabling a policy to fix a login problem at 6pm and never turning it back on

This is the gap SteelCurtain is built around. Not to catch you out — to make sure that when you tell a customer yes, it’s actually true.
