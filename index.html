Find and fix the data your app is leaving public.

Apps built fast, often with AI coding tools, ship Supabase tables with row-level security missing or too loose, so anyone with your public key can read them. Free scanners can tell you that. Buko fixes it: it checks your own schema, writes the policies that should be there, and stops a bad change before it ships.

The CLI and GitHub check are in early access. We’ll only use your email to tell you about Buko.

Finding the gap is free. Closing it correctly, and keeping it closed as your schema changes, is the work. That’s what Buko does.

What it fixes

The mistakes that turn a database behind your app into a database anyone can read.

Row-level security off
Tables your public key can reach with no rules at all, so every row is readable, and sometimes writable.
Rules that let too much through
Policies that only check that someone is signed in, or always say yes, instead of checking who owns the row.
Changes that reopen a hole
A new migration that adds a table without rules or loosens one that was fine.

How it works

  1. Point it at your project

    Run one command against your own Supabase project or its migrations. It reads your schema and policies, not your app’s code.

  2. It writes the fix

    For each table that’s open, it writes the row-level security policy that should be there, as a migration your team reviews and applies.

  3. It keeps it closed

    Add it as a check on your GitHub pull requests, and a change that opens a new hole fails before it ships. Next on the roadmap: ongoing monitoring, and backends beyond Supabase.

How we handle your data

Your database holds your customers’ data. Here’s what we commit to.

  • Only your own projects. Buko checks projects you own or are authorized to test, never anyone else’s.
  • Schema, not rows. The check looks at your tables and policies, not the customer data inside them.
  • You approve every change. Fixes arrive as migrations for you to review. Buko never changes your database on its own.
  • It runs where you work. A command-line tool on your machine and a check in your own GitHub repository.
